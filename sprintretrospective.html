What is a Sprint Retrospective activity?

The Sprint Retrospective is a meeting held at the end of each Sprint, after the Sprint Review and before the next Sprint begins. Its objective is to inspect how the team worked and find ways to improve its process. Instead of looking at the product, the team looks at its teamwork, tools, and communication. For example, a team might notice that pull requests sat unreviewed for two days and agree to review them every morning.

The whole Scrum team takes part: the Developers, the Scrum Master, and the Product Owner. The Scrum Master usually facilitates, making sure everyone can speak openly and the discussion stays constructive. A common format asks each person three questions: what worked well that we want to keep doing, what did not work well that we should stop doing, and what should we start doing or improve. The team then picks one or two improvements to try in the next Sprint.

The Sprint Retrospective is not a Sprint Review, since it does not demonstrate the product or collect stakeholder feedback. It is also not a blame session, a performance review of individuals, or a status report. For example, if a deadline was missed, the team asks what in the process caused the delay, not who is at fault. Holding this meeting every Sprint helps the team get a little better each time, which is the idea of continuous improvement.
