Who is Kenneth S. Rubin?

What does he know about Scrum?

Kenneth S. Rubin, commonly known as Kenny Rubin, is an experienced software development professional, author, and expert in Agile project management and Scrum. He has a background in computer science, earning a bachelor's degree in Information and Computer Science from the Georgia Institute of Technology and a master's degree in Computer Science from Stanford University. Throughout his career, Rubin has worked as a software developer, executive, consultant, and project manager. He has also served as the first managing director of the Scrum Alliance, an organization dedicated to promoting the adoption and effective use of Scrum. His experience working with organizations ranging from startups to large corporations has given him a broad understanding of how teams can use Agile practices to improve product development.

Rubin is particularly knowledgeable about Scrum, an Agile framework that helps teams manage complex projects by breaking work into smaller, manageable increments. He is the author of Essential Scrum: A Practical Guide to the Most Popular Agile Process, a book that explains Scrum's principles, roles, events, and artifacts. The book discusses important concepts such as the Product Owner, Scrum Master, and Developers, as well as the product backlog, sprint planning, daily Scrum meetings, sprint reviews, and retrospectives. Rubin explains how these elements work together to help teams organize their responsibilities, collaborate effectively, and deliver valuable products. His goal is to make Scrum understandable and practical for people who want to implement it successfully in real-world projects.

In addition to his writing, Rubin has extensive practical experience teaching and coaching organizations on Scrum and Agile methods. He has trained thousands of professionals and coached more than 200 companies, helping teams improve their development processes and adapt Scrum to their specific needs. His expertise comes from working with Scrum in different roles, including Product Owner, Scrum Master, development team member, and executive leader. This experience allows him to explain Scrum from both the team's perspective and the organization's broader business perspective. Overall, Rubin's work emphasizes that Scrum is more than a set of meetings or rules; it is a framework for encouraging teamwork, transparency, continuous improvement, and the frequent delivery of useful products.
