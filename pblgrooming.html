What is product backlog grooming?

Product backlog grooming, also called backlog refinement, is the ongoing activity where the Scrum team reviews and improves the items in the product backlog. The team adds new items, removes ones that are no longer needed, rewrites unclear descriptions, and puts items in priority order. The goal is to keep the backlog healthy so the most valuable work is always clear and ready to be picked up in the next Sprint.

During grooming, the Product Owner and the development team work together to make each item understandable and estimable. Large items are split into smaller ones, acceptance criteria are written, and story points are estimated, often with a technique like planning poker. The team also discusses dependencies, such as which items must be finished before others can start. Grooming is not a single meeting. It is continuous, and the team usually sets aside a small part of each Sprint for it.

For example, a team building a website might start with a vague item like "improve the site." Through grooming, it becomes specific items such as "Add a webpage on Daily Scrum" and "Move CSS into an external file," each with a user story, acceptance criteria, and an estimate. If the client later decides a feature is no longer needed, the team deletes it from the backlog, and if a new idea appears, they add it and rank it by value. Because of this regular cleanup, Sprint planning goes faster and the team always knows what to build next.
